# @keelwerk/module-members

Memberships, roles and invitations of one tenant. It builds on nothing at run time: `@keelwerk/module-accounts` and `@keelwerk/module-auth` are optional, and the module works beside either, both or neither.

- `members.membership` (history chain, data subject `subject`, unique per person and tenant): `role` (`owner`, `admin`, `member` or `viewer`, exactly one) and the `invitationId` it came from. Created by accepting an invitation only; the role history is the audit trail. The store keeps one live owner per tenant (`members__membership__role_uq`, a partial unique index on `role` where the role is `owner` and the row is not archived), so two accepts at once cannot make two owners; a pending owner invitation is unique the same way (`members__invitation__role_uq`).
- Roles: the enricher `members.roles` (a fill of the core's `principalEnrichers`) gives a signed-in person of a tenant the role of their live membership as `roles` and as one membership of kind `members.membership`. It runs once per session and again at every revalidation, so a role change shows at the next one. `hasRole(ctx, ...roles)` (this package's own helper, for the rules of this module) reads the memberships, never `roles`, so another enricher or a test principal that names a role grants nothing here.
- Rules: every member reads the members; admins and the owner change a role (never to owner, through `members.membership.changeRole`), remove a member (`members.membership.removeMember`) and read and issue invitations; the owner alone moves ownership. A deny rule keeps the owner row out of `update`, `archive`, `changeRole` and `removeMember`: such a call answers `not_found`, as a revoked API key does. The generated `update` and `archive` of a membership are open to no user. Ownership moves only through `members.membership.transferOwnership`, and the former owner becomes an admin. `invite`, `changeRole` and `removeMember` load the caller's live membership (admin or owner) and refuse with `forbidden` otherwise, as `transferOwnership` does for the owner: a long-lived connection is enriched once, so a person who was removed or demoted while their session stayed open can no longer invite, grant a role or remove anyone. The check guards against a stale session, not against a demotion that commits at the same instant.
- `members.invitation` (state history, data subject `subject`): `email` (sealed, found only by its keyed digest, unique among pending invitations), `role`, `status` (`pending`, `accepted`, `withdrawn`, `expired`), `invitedBy`, `publicId`, `tokenVerifier` and `expiresAt`. While pending the data subject is the invitation's own id; accepting moves it to the accepting person (the address is sealed again under the person), so an erasure of that person reaches the invitation and its address reads as absent afterwards. `email` is absent once an invitation ended without being accepted.
- `members.invitation.invite`: an admin or the owner invites an address as `admin`, `member` or `viewer`. An address with a pending, unexpired invitation is refused as a conflict that does not name it; an expired one is settled and replaced. The accept token `kwi_<public id>_<secret>` is shown once (`shownOnce`), only the verifier is stored, and neither the token nor the address reaches an event, receipt, job or log line.
- `members.invitation.createOwner`: bring-up. Only an operator may call it; a tenant with an owner refuses, and a repeat withdraws the earlier owner invitation, so one owner invitation is live at a time. The two owner indexes hold that when two calls run at the same moment on PostgreSQL: the store refuses the second writer, the unit repeats once and withdraws the first.
- `members.invitation.accept`: a signed-in person presents the token. It works once and for seven days; every failure (unknown, wrong, used, withdrawn, expired) is the one answer `members.invitation_invalid`, and an unknown token costs one verification like a known one. The address is not compared with the person's. A person who is already a member is refused, and so is an owner invitation in a tenant that has an owner.
- Withdrawing is the generated `update` of an invitation under the rule `members.invitation.withdraw`: only the inviter, only a pending invitation, only to `withdrawn`. The keyed timer `expire:<invitation id>` settles an expired invitation (a timer that decides a few milliseconds before the expiry sets itself again at the expiry); `accept` checks the expiry itself, so a late timer lets nothing through.
- Every end of an invitation, whichever way (accepted, expired, withdrawn, retired by `invite` or withdrawn by `createOwner`), is settled by the reaction `members.settleEndedInvitation` in the same unit: an invitation that ends without accept drops its address, and the kernel is asked to erase the invitation's id as a subject (`eraseOwnSubject`, section 14.7.5), so the worker shreds its key. The sealed address of the row and every sealed copy of it in the invitation's change records become unreadable; an ended, not accepted invitation then reads as an erased row (`not_found`). An accepted invitation keeps its address under the accepting person until the person is erased. A deny rule on `core.eraseSubject` that matches `members.system` (a hold) blocks every end of an invitation.
- Known limit: a pending invitation, at most seven days from its issue, keeps its address sealed under its own id, and the tenant's admins and owner read the address they entered, also after the person behind the address was erased in the meantime; the erasure of a person does not reach pending invitations to their address yet. An operator who knows the invitation's id can erase it by `core.eraseSubject` at any time.
- Erasing the owner's person archives the owner membership (and the slot of the index is free), so the tenant has no owner until an operator runs `createOwner` again.
- Delivery: the extension point `invitationDelivery` (a set) takes fillers that send the message. No filler ships in M2: without one `invite` and `createOwner` answer the token, which the caller hands on; with one the token goes to the filler only and the answer carries none.

`src/module.generated.ts` is produced by `pnpm manifests` and the files under `migrations/own/` by the CLI migrations producer; neither is edited by hand.
